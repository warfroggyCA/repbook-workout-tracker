# Product language

Repbook is for someone working out. Write as you would speak to that person.

- Lead with what happened and, when needed, what to do next.
- Use short sentences and familiar words: workout, set, target, saved, waiting to save, and missing details.
- Explain a limit specifically: “Not enough detail to compare” rather than “No comparable evidence.”
- Keep labels short. Put useful calculations and technical record details behind an optional disclosure.
- Do not expose storage, matching, or validation terminology in routine instructions. Keep internal identifiers and export field names unchanged.
- Be precise about unsaved work. “Kept on this device” is not the same as “Saved.” Do not promise successful saving before confirmation.
- Missing details do not mean zero, pain-free, completed, or safe. Say what is missing without a paragraph of caveats.
- Destructive actions still need clear scope and consequences. Shorten warnings without hiding permanent deletion or lost unsaved work.
- Generated Coach answers follow the same style. Existing user notes and saved Coach reviews are historical records; never rewrite them to match new copy.

Examples:

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| No comparable evidence | Not enough detail to compare |
| Waiting for save acknowledgement | Waiting to save |
| Evidence used | Based on |
| Retained package manifests | Saved exports |
| Acknowledged performed working sets appear first… | Your saved sets are shown below. Warm-ups are listed separately. |

Copy changes must preserve calculations, record states, and action behavior. Update affected label assertions and verify narrow-screen, error, and recovery flows in the running app.
